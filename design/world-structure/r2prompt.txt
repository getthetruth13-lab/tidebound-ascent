# Tidebound Ascent: World-Structure Brainstorm Brief (shared context for every model)

## The game
Tidebound Ascent is a dark, painterly drowned-world deck-building roguelike in the style of Slay the Spire (original names only, no StS/MTG terms). Target: Samsung S24 Ultra, landscape. Owner: Spencer (no game-dev background). Priorities: 1 fun, 2 visual impressiveness and environments ("each act and floor band should feel like a place you travel through"), 3 cohesion (one style, palette family, voice), 4 polish. Map is randomly generated every run. Naming voice: concrete and weathered (salt, rust, kelp, brass, tallow, rope, bone); Act 1 names from tidepool/kelp/old stone, Act 2 iron/rivets/harbour/garrison, Act 3 gale/cloud/static/hail, Final pale stone/lamp/gold. No high-fantasy boilerplate.

## The problem to solve (Spencer's words, paraphrased)
Act 1 is called "The Drowned Stair", but its backgrounds (a shore, a kelp cloister, a bell court, a sea cave for events, etc.) do not look like climbing stairs, and a branching map does not make sense on a staircase. It would make sense once we reach the tower and climb it in Acts 2 and 3. He wants a direction for Acts 1, 2 and 3 (and the Final) where map, backgrounds, enemies, events, names, story and cinematic all match the lore, so the player feels fully immersed at all times. Fix it now, before building more (only Act 1 is built and arted; Acts 2–3 are designed on paper only).

## Current lore (premise)
The sun did not set one evening. It sank beneath the Pale Sea and became the **Drowned Sun**; every year since, its pull has dragged the tide higher up the coasts. Then the sea drew back further than anyone living had seen, and the **Drowned Stair** rose out of the shallows: a sunken tower whose summit is the **Pale Lighthouse**. Relight its lamp and the sun will be called back up, so the old keepers swore. You climb because the tide is coming for everyone else. Old faction: the **Tidewardens** built the Bastion and the Maelstrom Engine to pull the tide down. Recurring NPCs: Quartermaster Wren (merchant, appears in every act), the Ferryman. Collectibles: three **Beacon Shards** (Tide Shard from a chest, Gale Shard from Galecrowned elites, etc.) unlock the Final act.

## Classes (why they climb)
- **Tidecaller** (crowned barnacled trident warden, teal): moon-sworn keeper of the tides; the sea answers them and they mean to make it obey again.
- **Echo Mage** (masked porcelain bell-mage, violet): heard the Leviathan Bell's last note from 40 leagues inland and follows the echo.
- **Cartographer** (stocky hat, spyglass, lantern, amber): wants to chart the one coast nobody has mapped and sell the only copy.
- **Duelist** (lean fencer, crimson scarf): swore an oath to the last lighthouse keeper to carry her blade to the lamp and light it.

=== ROUND 1: claude ===
# Claude's proposal: one tower, climbed from the sea to the sky

## The core fix

The tower is the whole world. The structure is a single vertical climb, and each act is a different part of the tower or its works. Act 1 is the **foot** of the tower, the drowned city that grew around it. That is why the map can branch: **a city has many streets, and a stair has one line.** Branching only makes sense where the climber chooses between routes. The map is a choice of routes up the **slopes**.

I would stop saying "the Drowned Stair" is the tower and keep it only as the name of the **spiral staircase inside**, the one line the climber is trying to reach. The **Pale Lighthouse** is the tower's top. The "Stair" becomes the Final's name and the goal of the whole game.

| | Place | Shape of travel |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | **The Drowned Shallows** (the Tower's foot) | Horizontal into vertical: wade, then climb terraces |
| Act 2 | **The Brine Bastion** (the Tidewardens' works built *into the tower's flank*) | Up through a fortress, harbour to ramparts |
| Act 3 | **The Galecrown** (the tower's upper shaft above the clouds) | Outside, on ledges and spires |
| Final | **The Pale Lighthouse** | The one true spiral: no branching, 4 nodes |

## Why the map branches

In every act the climber is choosing **routes through a broken place**: a collapsed gallery here, a flooded street there. A **story line in the map frame** (Act 1 chart: "the tide has broken the stair; find a way around") explains it. Short version for the UI: *"The Stair is broken here. Every road up is a gamble."*

The branches also link to **tide mechanics**: low tide opens one route, so the chart can mark flooded segments in teal (cosmetic first, mechanical later).

## Act 1: The Drowned Shallows (display rename; code id unchanged)

The act is about **leaving the water and finding the first step**. The Stair rises only at the end.

- **Floors 1–5, Wrack Shelf** (`a1_shore`, keep): the lowest tide in a generation. The tower stands far off, at the edge of the shelf, as a dark mass. *The climber is far away and low.*
- **Floors 6–10, Kelp Cloister → rename "The Sunken Terraces"** (`a1_cloister`): the drowned town built on the tower's lower slope, **terraced streets going up**. The foreground already has colonnades. Re-stage it as *one terrace of a flooded town seen from a higher step*, with the shallows visible **below** through arches.
- **Floors 11–14, Bellwater Court → "The Bell Stair Court"** (`a1_bellcourt`): the top terrace. The tower wall now **fills the right half**, and the first true stair (the entrance flight) rises into it.
- **Lair:** Belfry of the Leviathan Bell and Matriarch's Kelp Hollow stay. The bell hangs in the **gate-tower** that guards the entrance (Act 2 begins "Through the Bell Gate"). The Matriarch's pool is the **last flooded cistern** under the gate.

**Progress cue:** the sea level **drops** and the tower **grows** from band to band. This is the one rule every background follows (see below).

**Act 1 name:** I suggest **"The Drowned Shallows"** over "Sunken Terraces". It keeps the voice (salt, kelp) and promises the Stair as a payoff rather than a lie. The title card becomes *"Act I · The Drowned Shallows · Where the tide gave the tower back."* The line already works.

## Act 2: The Brine Bastion

The Tidewardens carved the Bastion **into the tower's flank** to hold back the sea. It is a fortress that is also a staircase, with levels linked by lifts, chains and gun-decks.

- **1–5 Outer Harbour:** the tower's foot is now the harbour wall; wrecks at anchor, sea at the climber's back.
- **6–10 Flooded Barracks:** inside the wall, the garrison still at their posts, water at knee level.
- **11–14 Gale-Lashed Ramparts:** the top of the works, open to the weather. The first real wind and the first clouds *below* the horizon.
- **Bosses:** Tidewarden Colossus (sluice gate) and Wreck Queen (the harbour basin). The basin should be **seen from above** in the Rampart band, so the climber looks down on the harbour they crossed.
- **Rooms:** events in the **Drowned Archive** (the Wardens' records); shop is the **Chandlery**, Wren's second stall (keep); camp on a gun deck; chest is an armoury strongbox.

Act 2's transition becomes less magical: instead of a waterspout, the climber takes a **rope-and-chain lift** the Wardens built, which snaps halfway, and the climber is flung into cloud. The waterspout can stay as the villain's force, not the vehicle.

## Act 3: The Galecrown

The shaft of the tower above the clouds, wind-scoured, hung with the wrecks of ships the storm lifted.

- **1–5 Cloudbreak Cliffs → "Cloudbreak Ledges"** (the tower's own outer ledges, not natural cliffs).
- **6–10 Thunder Spires:** broken pinnacles and lightning rods.
- **11–14 Crown Terraces:** the **Upturned Sea** hangs overhead; water falling *upward* in sheets.
- **Rooms:** events at the Windshrine; shop is the stranded hull (Wren arrives by sky-barge); camp lee-side; chest a Gale Reliquary.
- **Bosses:** Thalassa the Undrowned and the Maelstrom Engine, where the Engine is the Wardens' last machine, bridging Act 2's story.

## Final: The Pale Lighthouse

The **only literal stair**, a spiral inside the white shaft. Four nodes: camp, bazaar, wardens, then the Drowned Sun. The Drowned Sun is **seen through the lantern glass from the first node**, a dim glow far below on the sea floor, brighter each step. It reads as the payoff of the "sun sank" premise: you have climbed so high that you can finally **look down on it**.

## The rule for every background (one-sentence art brief)

> Every scene contains **three readable elements**: the **tower** (size grows by band), the **sea level** (drops by band), and **a visible way up** (steps, ramp, chain, ladder).

Act 1 bands: tower tiny and distant → tower half the frame → tower fills the right with steps entering it. Sea: wide below → narrow strip → only glimpsed through arches.

=== ROUND 1: codex ===
**Make the Drowned Stair the name of the entire ascent: a stepped coastal citadel built around one immense stone spine, with the Pale Lighthouse at its summit.** It has terraces, streets, harbour works, galleries and stairs. From the sea, its overlapping structures resemble a single ruined tower. Within it, several routes climb toward the same gates.

Keep Act 1 named **The Drowned Stair**. Its identity is the newly exposed lower precinct of that larger structure. This preserves the approved cinematic, Lamplight and most existing art while giving the branching map a physical explanation.

The key visual rule is **show elevation through relationships**: the waterline drops, retaining walls become taller, familiar landmarks appear below, and the lighthouse becomes clearer. Steps help, but adding steps to every foreground would not make the world convincing.

**The physical journey and branching map**

The citadel occupies a steep island ridge. Its builders cut terraces into the rock and extended them with massive stone foundations. The Tidewardens later fortified its middle levels and installed machinery in its crown.

The player climbs through four connected places:

- Exposed tidal terraces at the foot of the ridge.
- A fortified harbour and garrison built into its middle.
- Storm-battered maintenance works around its upper spine.
- The lighthouse’s final stair and lantern gallery.

Each map row represents a stage of ascent, not one literal storey. Nodes are places reached along alternative routes: a quay, passage, garden, guardroom or sheltered landing. Connections are traversable streets, stairs, bridges and galleries. Routes split around collapsed structures and converge at surviving crossings.

Keep the existing random map rules. The geography explains their shape; it does not require movement puzzles, tide timers or a second navigation system.

**Act I — The Drowned Stair**

*Floors 1–5: Wrack Steps.* The retreating sea exposes the arrival quay, tidal shelves and lowest retaining walls. Dawn catches wet stone; kelp hangs above the current waterline. The player starts close enough to the sea to hear loose boats scraping rock.

Keep the shoreline’s openness. Its architectural purpose becomes clearer through a visible landing, a rising route toward the tower mass, and masonry partly buried beneath wrack.

*Floors 6–10: Kelp Cloister.* These are the lower keepers’ quarters, built around stepped gardens and covered walks. Floodwater remains trapped in their basins. Broken roofs admit green light through hanging kelp. The player crosses upper galleries while looking into flooded courts below.

The cloister can contain flat rooms. A journey uphill naturally passes through rooms between flights of stairs.

*Floors 11–14: Bellwater Court.* The routes converge on a raised ceremonial terrace beneath the Bell Gate. Rain replaces dawn. The arrival shore is now visible far below through breaches in the parapet. The lighthouse remains dark above the Bastion.

*Lair: Leviathan Belfry or Matriarch’s Cistern.* Both occupy the gate terrace. The Leviathan Bell hangs in the gatehouse; the Kelp Matriarch has rooted in an adjacent keeper’s cistern and spread across the passage.

Both bosses lead to the same exit. If the Matriarch is fought, the bell remains an ominous landmark rather than an implied second compulsory boss.

Enemy themes remain barnacles, scavengers, kelp and drowned masonry. Stone sentinels and ruined guards belong here alongside shore creatures: this was an inhabited precinct reclaimed by the sea.

Events occur in recesses, terraces and surviving rooms. Wren shelters in a wrecked boat lodged against a quay wall. Camps occupy dry keeper’s alcoves. The row-eight chest sits on an old storage landing above the exposed shore. These places support repeated discoveries without suggesting that every room contains the same cave.

**Act II — The Brine Bastion**

The Bastion is the citadel’s fortified middle, built by the Tidewardens around a **high harbour basin connected to the sea by monumental locks**. Ships entered through successive chambers before the machinery failed. The stranded fleet now explains the Wreck Queen without placing a normal sea-level harbour halfway up the ascent.

*Floors 1–5: Lockside Harbour.* The Bell Gate opens onto the basin’s lower service quay. Rusted lock doors tower overhead. Ship hulls lean against masonry; chain lifts and gun platforms connect the routes. Through an open spillway, the lower terraces lie beneath the player.

*Floors 6–10: Flooded Barracks.* Routes climb through garrison blocks, pump rooms and covered bridges. The flooding comes from trapped basin water and leaking conduits. Outside windows, the harbour appears progressively lower.

*Floors 11–14: Gale-Lashed Ramparts.* The player reaches the upper defensive ring. Guns face the pale horizon. Rain blows sideways through embrasures; the storm crown dominates the skyline.

*Lair: Main Sluice or Fleet Basin.* The Tidewarden Colossus guards the upper sluice crossing. The Wreck Queen occupies the elevated fleet basin beside it. Either route opens onto the same maintenance ascent.

Enemies express a garrison that continued working after death: divers, gunners, guards, machinery and salt-haunted crews. Events belong in the Drowned Archive and abandoned duty rooms. Wren’s Harbour Chandlery sells salvaged stores. Camps use sheltered gun decks; chests are armoury strongboxes.

Revise “Through the Bell Gate” so the harbour **opens ahead**, with the drowned terraces below. It should not suggest a descent into a separate coastal town.

**Act III — The Galecrown**

The Galecrown is the exposed upper spine and the Tidewardens’ failed weather works. The Maelstrom Engine was installed here to draw water through the citadel’s conduits and force the tide down. Its failure helped gather the **Upturned Sea**, a suspended mass of seawater turning above the ridge.

*Floors 1–5: Cloudbreak Ledges.* Narrow exterior paths and maintenance bridges rise from the Bastion. Clouds interrupt views of the roofs below. Stone is scoured clean; vegetation survives only in sheltered cracks.

*Floors 6–10: Thunder Spires.* Lightning rods, broken conductors and pump towers stand among the natural stone teeth. Storm creatures swim through airborne spray. Branches follow different service bridges around damaged installations.

*Floors 11–14: Crown Terraces.* The player reaches the engine’s upper platforms. The Upturned Sea occupies much of the sky, its underside streaked with foam and drifting wreckage. The Pale Lighthouse is finally visible across a broken crown bridge.

*Lair: Engine Crown or Suspended Basin.* The Maelstrom Engine is encountered at its exposed heart. Thalassa the Undrowned inhabits the adjacent basin beneath the hanging water. Both guard access to the lighthouse crossing.

Use gale, hail, static and storm-tossed sea life as the enemy vocabulary. Events occur in wind shelters and abandoned maintenance stations. Wren has lashed a small wrecked hull to a service platform; the shop’s arrival is credible because the storm lifts wreckage here. Camps occupy the lee of stone buttresses; chests are sealed instrument reliquaries.

Keep “The Updraft” as a cinematic traversal through a broken lift shaft: the climber catches the waterspout’s rising current and lands on a visible maintenance ledge. It bridges a specific gap in the same structure.

**Final — The Pale Lighthouse**

The lighthouse was the original summit structure. The later fortifications and machinery were built beneath it.

The three Beacon Shards are **Tide, Brass and Gale**: surviving pieces of the lamp’s focusing assembly, recovered from the three precincts. Keep the Tide chest and Gale elite sources; establish the Brass source in Act II later. Present them as guaranteed discoveries along successful progression, so the ending does not depend on unexplained luck.

The shards restore the broken beacon crossing for “The Beacon Path.” Inside, the four-node sequence has clear physical locations: a sheltered keeper’s landing, Wren’s last supply table, the wardens at the lantern door, and the gallery.

The lamp calls the Drowned Sun upward. Its presence rises through the glass-black water surrounding the crown, filling the gallery’s windows with submerged gold. **Lighting the lamp begins the final confrontation.** The climber must hold the beacon against the Sun’s pull long enough for it to rise.

Victory reveals the same coast shown at the beginning, illuminated from above. Water begins draining from the terraces. The payoff is immediately legible: the journey restored light to the world the player crossed.

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Solution

### Spencer’s Problem
Act 1 is called **"The Drowned Stair"**, yet its backgrounds read as flat shores, interior halls, and open courtyards. A branching map feels unnatural on a single narrow staircase. Furthermore, the transitions between Act 1 (flooded stair), Act 2 (horizontal harbour fortress), and Act 3 (cliffside clouds via a waterspout updraft) create geographical disconnect.

### The Unified Solution: The Monolithic Spire-Mountain
The "Drowned Stair" is **not a narrow interior flight of steps** in a slim tower. It is a **colossal, ancient monolithic spire-mountain** sculpted by precursor keepers into thousands of monumental terraced steps, causeways, and landings leading up to the **Pale Lighthouse**. 

Because each tier of the Stair is hundreds of yards wide—consisting of outer seawall ledges, flooded colonnades, drainage grottos, and inner stair-wells—a **branching 15-row map makes total sense**. The climber isn't picking which stair tread to step on; they are selecting which **causeway, gallery, or breach-passage** to scale to reach the next terrace level.

```
       [ FINAL ACT ] ------ The Pale Lighthouse (Summit / Lamp Gallery)
            ^
       [  ACT 3   ] ------ The Galecrown (Upper Terraces & Sky Viaducts)
            ^
       [  ACT 2   ] ------ The Brine Bastion (Garrison Terraces & Sluice Gates)
            ^
       [  ACT 1   ] ------ The Drowned Stair (Submerged Basal Terraces)
```

By defining the entire physical world as one continuous, upward-scaling structure, we preserve every existing asset and code ID, fix the branching logic instantly, and give each act a clear place in a single vertical climb.

# Round 1 — Grok Bot (facilitator) proposal

## Core idea: the Drowned Stair is a mountain, not a staircase
The Drowned Stair is a colossal sea-mount carved into a terraced holy city by the old keepers: thousands of stairways, landings, cloisters, bridges and tunnels winding up one mountain-tower to the Pale Lighthouse at its peak. "The Stair" is what sailors call the whole thing, the way people call a city "the Hill". The map branches because there are many ways up: a broad processional stair, a collapsed side gallery, a flooded street, a rope-bridge between terraces. Each map row is one **landing** (a level of the city); each path is a **route**. The tide drains and refloods daily, so routes open and close: that is why the map is different every run.
