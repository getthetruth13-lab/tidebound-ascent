# Tidebound Ascent: World Structure, Final Direction
*Agreed by Claude, Codex (ChatGPT), Gemini, Grok Build and Grok Bot, Oct 10 2026. Design only: nothing in the game has been changed yet.*

## One-page summary
**The big idea:** the Drowned Stair isn't one staircase. It's a whole drowned island-mountain that the old lighthouse keepers carved into a giant terraced stone city, with the Pale Lighthouse on top. Sailors call the whole thing "the Stair", the way people call a hilly town "the Hill". From out at sea it looks like one huge tower, so the intro cinematic still tells the truth.

**Why the map branches:** each terrace (level) of the city is hundreds of yards wide, and the main staircase has collapsed. Every node on the map is a dry spot on a level, like a street, a colonnade, a landing or a grotto. Every path is a way up that's still standing, like a side stair, a bridge or a gallery. The tide floods different routes every time, which is why the map is different each run. A short line on the Act 1 map says so: *"The great flight is broken. Every road up is a gamble."*

**The acts, bottom to top, all on the same mountain:**
- **Act I · The Wrack Terraces** (renamed from "The Drowned Stair"): the lowest levels, just uncovered by the tide. Subtitle: *"The foot of the Drowned Stair."*
- **Act II · The Brine Bastion:** the Tidewardens' fortress around the mountain's middle, built around a high harbour that ships used to reach through giant locks.
- **Act III · The Galecrown:** the storm-wrapped upper levels above the clouds, with the Upturned Sea hanging overhead.
- **Final · The Pale Lighthouse:** the only real, single staircase in the game, spiralling up inside the lighthouse. That's why this act has no branches.

**One rule for every background:** the sea gets lower, the lighthouse gets closer, and there's always a visible way up.

**What it costs now:** a few text renames, 5 of the 10 Act 1 backgrounds retouched with Grok Imagine edits, 5 kept as they are, none fully redone. The map parchment also gets retouched, plus one cinematic line and one cinematic image. Code changes are text only: no ids, rules or balance change.

## 1. The journey, explained simply
1. **The sun sank**, and every year its pull dragged the sea higher (intro scenes 1–2).
2. **The sea drew back**, and the mountain-city the keepers built rose out of the water. That's the Drowned Stair (scene 3).
3. **You row to its foot** (the "Rowing to the Stair" travel scene) and start climbing its lowest levels: **Act I**.
4. You pass through the **Bell Gate** into the Tidewardens' fortress: **Act II**.
5. You ride the Tidewardens' **chain-lift** up through the clouds into the storm: **Act III**.
6. With three Beacon Shards, a bridge of light carries you to the **Pale Lighthouse**: **Final**. You climb its spiral stair, light the lamp, and look down at the black sea, where the **Drowned Sun** rises to meet you.

**How the climb feels:** first the sea is at your elbow, then you look down at it, then you look down at the clouds, and finally you look down at the Drowned Sun. The ending works because you've been rising the whole game.

**Lamplight fits as is:** "your lantern burns on the landing where you fell" is now literally true, because the city is made of landings. Death text names the place, like *"The tide took you on the Kelp Colonnade."* The Stair Climb track, the Keeper's Ledger and "the stair remembers" all stay. Later, the Act 1→2 travel scene shows past climbers' lanterns lining the Bell Gate, so the Lamplight story appears in the world itself.

**Rooms in this world:**
- **Events (? rooms)** are side places on the levels: grottos under terraces, chapels, recesses and flooded houses. These are the detours the tide forces on you.
- **Merchant:** Quartermaster Wren's boat-hull stall, wedged against a terrace wall. She keeps hauling it up the mountain.
- **Camps:** dry alcoves on the landings.
- **Chests:** things the tide left stranded on stone landings.

**Enemies and bosses:** no changes. Act 1's barnacle, kelp and drowned-stone creatures are what the sea left in the city's houses. Act 2 is the rust and rivets of the drowned garrison, Act 3 is storm creatures, and the Final is pale-stone wardens.

**Transitions:**
- **Act 1→2, "Through the Bell Gate":** keep.
- **Act 2→3, now "The Warden's Lift":** a Tidewarden chain-lift climbs the mountain's side through the clouds into violet storm light. It replaces the waterspout, which only made sense after one of the two bosses. Small detail: it shows a hint of whichever boss you just beat.
- **Act 3→Final, "The Beacon Path":** keep.
- **Victory, "The Lighthouse Lit":** keep.

## 5. Where the models disagreed and how it was settled
1. **Act 1's name.** Round 1: Codex, Gemini and Grok Bot said keep "The Drowned Stair". Claude suggested "The Drowned Shallows" and Grok Build "The Wrack Terraces". In Round 2, Claude and Gemini switched to "The Wrack Terraces", and Codex still preferred keeping the old name. Tie-break: everyone accepted **"The Wrack Terraces"** with the subtitle "The foot of the Drowned Stair", so the word Stair still appears on screen and the whole climb keeps that name. Grok Bot changed its mind to agree.
2. **Act 2→3 transition.** The waterspout only made sense after the Colossus fight. Everyone accepted a chain-lift that works after either boss ("The Warden's Lift"). Claude added the idea of showing a hint of the boss you just beat.
3. **How many backgrounds to retouch.** Codex and Gemini wanted 8, Claude 5, Grok Build 4. We settled on 5, because those are the scenes that actually read wrong.
4. **Steps in every scene.** Gemini first wanted stairs added to every background. Codex's "show height through what you can see" won: stairs only where they make sense, otherwise a view down through an arch or window.
5. **Extra design ideas** (Codex's new Brass Shard, the lamp lighting starting the final boss fight, and many renames) were left out for now, so this decision stays small.
